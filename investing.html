The thesis

Follow the capex

The hyperscalers (the companies building the world's AI data centers) are spending on compute at a scale the market has never seen. I don't try to pick which AI model wins. I look at who gets paid no matter who wins: the companies that make the chips, package them, build them into servers, and power and cool the racks.


Taiwan sits at the center of that supply chain. It has the leading-edge foundry, advanced packaging, and many of the server makers that assemble AI racks. That's where most of my portfolio is.

How I map it

The AI supply chain, layer by layer

01

Hyperscaler capex

Where the money comes from. Cloud and AI capex guidance is the first thing I check every earnings season, because everything below depends on it.

02

Chip design: GPUs & custom ASICs

Merchant GPUs and in-house accelerators. More custom silicon means more design-service and foundry demand, not less.

03

Foundry & advanced packaging

Leading-edge nodes and CoWoS-style packaging are the bottleneck. When packaging capacity is tight, it decides how many accelerators ship.

04

Memory: HBM

High-bandwidth memory is stacked next to every accelerator. Its supply and pricing are a key read on how strong AI demand really is.

05

Servers & racks (ODMs)

Taiwanese ODMs turn chips into full AI servers and racks. Their monthly revenue reports are one of the fastest real-time signals of AI demand.

06

The picks & shovels

Substrates and PCBs, networking and optics, power supplies, and liquid cooling. Every new GPU generation needs more of all of them per rack.

Process

How I actually invest

Read

Sell-side to signal

Working on a global markets desk taught me to go through a lot of multilingual research quickly and pull out the few points that matter. I use the same habit on my own portfolio.

Track

Monthly data

Taiwanese companies report revenue every month. I track the supply chain names against capex guidance so I can see a demand shift before quarterly earnings confirm it.

Size

Concentrated, not reckless

It's a focused portfolio, so I think about position sizing, how correlated these names are (they all ride the same cycle), and what would make me wrong.

Review

Write it down

Like the 10+ earnings memos I wrote at work, I keep notes on why I own each position, so I review my original thesis and not the share price.

Risks

What keeps me honest

  • Capex digestion. If hyperscalers pause spending, the whole chain feels it at the same time.
  • Concentration. One theme and one region means a single shock can hit everything I own.
  • Geopolitics & export controls. Cross-Strait and U.S.–China policy risk is real for Taiwanese names.
  • FX. A USD-based investor holding TWD assets carries currency risk on top of stock risk.
